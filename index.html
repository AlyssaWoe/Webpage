STORYLINE

The Full Musical Narrative Created by Lin-Manuel Miranda

1776 Act I • Scene 1 & 2
"Alexander Hamilton"

The Ten-Dollar Founding Father Without A Father

Born out of wedlock in the Caribbean island of Nevis, orphaned as a boy, and surviving a devastating hurricane through his sheer gift of writing, nineteen-year-old Alexander Hamilton arrives in New York City with fiery intellect and unmatched ambition. In lower Manhattan, he meets the cautious, well-connected Aaron Burr, who advises him to "talk less, smile more." Hamilton dismisses this cautious restraint, seeking immediate revolution.

"There's a million things I haven't done, but just you wait, just you wait!"
1776 Act I • Scene 3 & 4
"My Shot"

The Sons of Liberty & "My Shot"

In a vibrant tavern, Hamilton forms an unbreakable brotherhood with three passionate abolitionists and revolutionaries: John Laurens, the fiery Marquis de Lafayette, and tailor-spy Hercules Mulligan. Together, they pledge to lay down their lives for American independence. Across the Atlantic, King George III scoffs at the colonists' rebellion in a darkly comedic pop ballad, predicting that America will crawl back to his rule.

"I am not throwing away my shot! Hey yo, I'm just like my country: I'm young, scrappy, and hungry!"
1780 Act I • Scene 5 & 10
"The Schuyler Sisters" & "Helpless"

The Winter's Ball & The Schuyler Sisters

The wealthy and progressive daughters of General Philip Schuyler—Angelica, Eliza, and Peggy—visit Manhattan eager to be part of the revolutionary movement. At a winter military ball, Eliza Schuyler falls instantly and deeply in love with Alexander ("Helpless"). Angelica, despite having an intense intellectual spark with Hamilton herself, introduces Alexander to Eliza, sacrificing her own heart to secure her sister’s eternal happiness ("Satisfied"). Alexander and Eliza marry shortly after.

"To the groom, to the bride, from your sister who is always by your side... At least I keep his eyes in my life."
1781 Act I • Scene 18 & 20
"Yorktown (The World Turned Upside Down)"

Washington's Command & Victory at Yorktown

General George Washington enlists Hamilton as his primary aide-de-camp and indispensable speechwriter ("Right Hand Man"). Though Hamilton repeatedly begs for battlefield command to prove his honor, Washington protects him until the climactic siege of Yorktown in 1781. Reunited with Lafayette, Laurens, and Mulligan, Hamilton commands a key redoubt assault, forcing General Cornwallis to surrender. The world is turned upside down as America claims independence.

"The world turned upside down! Freedom at small cost... We won, we won, we won!"
1787 Act I • Finale
"Non-Stop"

The Constitutional Convention & "Non-Stop"

Following the war, Alexander Hamilton and Aaron Burr return to New York as prominent defense attorneys. Hamilton is devastated by the news that John Laurens died in a pointless post-war skirmish. Refusing to rest, Hamilton champions the new U.S. Constitution and writes an astounding 51 of the 85 essays comprising the Federalist Papers. Newly inaugurated President George Washington appoints Hamilton as America’s first Secretary of the Treasury, setting up the political battles to come.

"Why do you write like you're running out of time? Write day and night like you're running out of time?"
1789 Act II • Scene 1 & 2
"What'd I Miss?" & "Cabinet Battle #1"

Jefferson Returns & The Cabinet Battles

Thomas Jefferson returns from his ambassadorship in Paris, oozing charm and Southern agrarian idealism ("What'd I Miss?"). When Hamilton proposes a bold federal bank plan to assume state debts and establish national credit, Jefferson and James Madison vehemently oppose it. President Washington moderates a fiery hip-hop cabinet debate, warning Hamilton that without Southern congressional votes, his financial visionary program will perish.

"Such a blunder, sometimes it makes me wonder why I even bring the thunder!"
1790 Act II • Scene 5
"The Room Where It Happens"

The Compromise of 1790: The Room Where It Happens

Behind closed doors over a private dinner, Hamilton strikes a historic bargain with Jefferson and Madison: the capital of the United States will be relocated to the South (the Potomac River, Washington D.C.) in exchange for Southern approval of Hamilton’s national banking system. Observing from the outside, Aaron Burr realizes that pure political neutrality has left him powerless, and he vows to seize power by any means necessary.

"No one else was in the room where it happened. The room where it happened, the room where it happened!"
1797 Act II • Scene 13 & 15
"The Reynolds Pamphlet" & "Burn"

The Reynolds Affair & Eliza's Heartbreak ("Burn")

Confronted by Jefferson, Madison, and Burr over suspicious financial disbursements, Hamilton fears he is being accused of government treason. To defend his public honor, he publishes the sensational 95-page Reynolds Pamphlet, publicly confessing to an adulterous blackmail affair with Maria Reynolds. The scandal ruins his political future. Devastated by the public humiliation, Eliza burns all their love letters, erasing herself from his narrative so historians can never judge her grief.

"I hope that you burn... You have no right to my heart. You do not get to say."
1801 Act II • Scene 17 & 18
"It's Quiet Uptown"

Philip's Tragic Duel & Forgiveness in Harlem

Hamilton’s eldest son Philip challenges George Eacker to a duel to defend his father’s honor and is fatally shot. Alexander and Eliza are engulfed in unbearable agony. Retreating to their countryside home in Harlem, Alexander walks quietly by Eliza’s side, begging for nothing and offering true repentance. In one of musical theatre’s most moving moments, Eliza takes his hand, choosing forgiveness through love.

"There are moments that the words don't reach. There is suffering too terrible to name... Forgiveness. Can you imagine?"
1804 Act II • Scene 22 & 23
"The World Was Wide Enough" & "Who Lives, Who Dies"

The Duel at Weehawken & Eliza's Legacy

In the deadlocked Presidential Election of 1800, Hamilton endorses his fierce ideological rival Thomas Jefferson over Aaron Burr, stating that "Jefferson has beliefs; Burr has none." Burr demands satisfaction in a duel across the Hudson River in Weehawken, New Jersey. Hamilton intentionally fires his pistol toward the sky, but Burr shoots him through the ribs. Hamilton dies hours later, leaving Burr to lament: "The world was wide enough for both Hamilton and me." In the emotional finale, Eliza lives for another fifty years, preserving Hamilton’s writings and founding New York City’s first private orphanage.

"Who lives, who dies, who tells your story? I speak out against slavery; you could have done so much more if you only had time."

About Hamilton

Alexander Hamilton is an award-winning musical with book, music, and lyrics by Lin-Manuel Miranda. It won eleven Tony Awards, the 2016 Pulitzer Prize for Drama, and the Grammy Award for Best Musical Theater Album.
